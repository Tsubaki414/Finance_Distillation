# August 2026 Employment Report: Structural Pressures Amid Market Resilience

The establishment survey reported that nonfarm payrolls rose by 162,000 in August, indicating that while total employment remains robust, the momentum is weakening.

When viewed over a longer horizon, the average monthly gain over the prior 12 months was 31,000, suggesting that while the current increase is strong, its sustainability warrants caution.

The establishment survey data reveals structural divergence: food services and drinking places added 59,000, and health care added 13,000, demonstrating resilience in the service sector.

Conversely, information employment fell by 23,000, and the number working part time for economic reasons fell by 414,000, implying that firms are shifting cost pressures onto reduced hours or fewer full-time positions.

Price and hours data show that average hourly earnings rose 0.3 percent on the month and average hourly earnings rose 3.1 percent from a year earlier, signaling strong wage growth that may constrain further rate cuts.

Revisions and participation rates alter the interpretation of raw job gains; the prior two months were revised up by a combined 55,000, yet the labor force participation rate was 61.6 percent and is down 0.5 percentage points since January, indicating a contraction in labor supply.

A counter-argument suggests the labor market has not genuinely weakened, as the unemployment rate was 4.1 percent at historical lows, and local government education added 42,000 provided a buffer.

The prevailing view maintains that the divergence between wage growth and reduced part-time employment suggests firms are controlling costs while struggling to sustain full-time roles, confirming the macroeconomic transition.

The release notes that the establishment survey measures nonfarm employment, hours, and earnings by industry, while the household survey measures labor force status, including unemployment, by demographic characteristics. _[background added for this audience]_

The release notes that the household survey counts people while the establishment survey counts payroll jobs, and that one should not subtract across these surveys. _[background added for this audience]_

The unemployment rate was 4.1 percent, and the threshold for invalidating the overheating narrative is a significant decline in private nonfarm average weekly hours or the unemployment rate falling below 4.1 percent.
